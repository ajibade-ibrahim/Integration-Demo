Identity resolution

One real company can arrive as a customer record from a CRM, another from Stripe and another from a billing system. Resolution is the per-owner pass that groups those unrelated rows into one CustomerAccount, keeping the source records untouched and every field attributable.

Role
Partition one owner's customers into company profiles and reconcile their fields.
Called by
resolveAfterSync() after a refresh; deletion and crosswalk withdrawal also trigger it.
Calls
evidence.ts, survivorship.ts, identity-repository.ts.
Key entry
resolveIdentities()

Key files

FileResponsibility
identity-resolver.ts The pass itself: load a tenant's customers, build evidence, assign accounts, apply assertions, report an outcome.
evidence.ts Turns customer and contact fields into match keys; builds tenant-aware foreign scopes.
normalize.ts Normalizes emails, domains and names before they become evidence.
free-email-domains.ts Refuses consumer mailbox domains as website evidence.
survivorship.ts Chooses each combined field: highest-priority non-null, earliest createDate, conflicts and losing values retained.
provenance.ts Reads the stored record of which source supplied each displayed field.
source-account.ts Captures and records the provider account a credential points at.
crosswalk.ts Parses imported explicit pairs (max 5000) for cases names and emails cannot join.
assertions.ts Human verdicts — same and distinct — and the canonical grouping of same pairs.
withdrawal.ts Forgets imported crosswalk pairs and reassesses the affected accounts.
resolve-after-sync.ts Runs the pass without ever failing the sync that preceded it.
mapping-retarget.ts Pure decisions for a one-off migration of mapping defaults. Not runtime logic.

How it fits

  • Resolution is per owner, not per integration. A single connection cannot see that the same company exists in three of them, so the pass runs across all of an owner's records after a sync.
  • Evidence has kinds and precedence. An explicit cross-reference is strongest, then a shared company email, then a shared website domain, then a shared contact email. Free mailbox domains are refused as domain evidence, or every Gmail customer would become one company.
  • Keys remember their source. A key records the provider account, the customer or contact row that offered it, and when it was last seen. That is what makes re-keying and deletion decidable without guessing.
  • Assertions outrank inference. “Same company” and “not this company” are read by the resolver and never written by it, so a human verdict survives every later pass. same pairs are processed as a unit, not one member at a time, which is what keeps the outcome independent of row order.
  • Survivorship is per field. The highest-priority connection with a non-null value wins; a blank never beats a present value. Creation date is the exception: the earliest reported date wins.
  • Losing values are kept. Provenance names the winning source record, field, value and observation time, and retains the losers with the reason they lost — including ordinary priority losses, not only ties.
  • The resolver never merges on a guess. If two accounts both claim a customer's keys, it picks a home deterministically and reports the rest as key_claimed_elsewhere; it does not re-parent anyone else.
  • Absence does not retire a key. A key that stops being offered stays put, because splitting a company on a temporary omission is worse than a stale join. Deletion and explicit withdrawal are the retirement paths.

Rules worth knowing

  • Total. Every customer ends with a non-null customerAccountId; one with no usable key gets a singleton.
  • Idempotent. A second pass over unchanged data creates no account, moves no customer and inserts no key. It only refreshes lastSeenAt.
  • Order-independent. Shuffling the input rows yields the same partition. That is why loading has a canonical order and same groups are handled as a unit.
  • A distinct verdict is never overridden by an inferred key. It is the one input the resolver will not revise.
  • Priority never changes membership. It decides whose version of a field is displayed, not which records are grouped.
  • Failure is contained. resolveAfterSync() logs and swallows a resolver error; the sync already succeeded, and the next pass simply retries.

Open this when…

  • Two records for the same company are not appearing under one account.
  • Two unrelated companies have been grouped together.
  • A combined field shows an unexpected source or value.
  • A human “same” or “not this company” verdict is not being honoured.
  • Deleting a connection or importing a crosswalk changed account membership.
  • The Accounts screen shows a field conflict.
